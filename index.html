Our Heritage

A loom hums in Banaras.
A story begins again.

For thirty-eight years, the same five families in a quiet lane off the Ganges have woven for GreenWeave. We do not chase trend; we honour technique — meena work that took our grandmother eleven months, kadhua weaves where every motif is born on the loom itself, never embroidered later.

Every saree leaves our atelier with the weaver's signature stitched into the inside fall. You'll find it where only you will look — a small intimacy between maker and wearer.

— Ananya Verma
Founder & Custodian, GreenWeave
The Process

From silk-worm to shoulder.

Four chapters of patient making, each guided by a master who learnt at their father's loom.

01

Yarn Spinning

Mulberry cocoons are reeled by hand, every silk thread tested for strand strength and lustre before reaching the warp board.

7 — 14 Days
02

Natural Dyeing

Roots, marigold, indigo, lac — pigments are simmered in copper vessels by dyers whose recipes have not changed in five generations.

3 — 6 Days
03

Loom Weaving

Pit-loom or pedal loom, kadhua or jaal — a single saree can take a master 40 to 240 days, motif by motif, prayer by prayer.

40 — 240 Days
04

Finishing & Signing

Each saree is inspected against daylight, lightly steamed, and signed inside the fall by the weaver who gave it her year.

2 — 3 Days
